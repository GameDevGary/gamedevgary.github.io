Story

My Dev Journey

I started playing video games when I was 5 years old. The first games I remember playing are Legend of Zelda, Spyro, Crash Bandicoot, Mario, and Street Fighter. I'm 30 now, and I've learned how much creativity, curiosity, and persistence shape the work you make.

Started with googling in 2010, "how to make video games". Options at the time were licensing an expensive engine like Unreal or something, working at a company, or writing my own engine from scratch. I wanted to learn by doing, so I started teaching myself.

I googled C#, found a crash course, and taught myself programming. Downloaded Unity, started learning about 2D/3D scenes and how game objects and code go together and how to build them. That practical, chaotic learning process taught me far more than any single course ever could.

Learning game development or any kind of art or project you create with tools and your mind is the best way to learn anything. Learning how to teach yourself is the best skill to learn, especially in a creative field where the best tools and workflows are always changing.

From the time I started playing video games, to the time I started making them, to now, everything I studied and learned has changed so much in ways that were always impossible to predict. That constant evolution is part of what keeps this work exciting.
